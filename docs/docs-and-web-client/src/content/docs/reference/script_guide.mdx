Every script Lipi Lekhika supports, and the few places where one behaves differently from what you might expect. Script names and codes for the API are on [Supported Scripts](/reference/supported_scripts).

The examples write **भगवद्गीता** (Bhagavad Gītā) in each script. Under each script name, **typing map** shows the keys for each letter and **compare** sets it beside a related script, letter by letter.

## Lipi Lekhika's own schemes

Two schemes for writing Indian scripts in Latin letters, made by Lipi Lekhika. Unlike the [standard schemes](#standard-romanization-schemes), which were designed for Sanskrit, they keep every distinction of every supported script, such as the short and long e and o of the South Indian scripts and the Tamil ழ ற ன.

| Script | Letters | भगवद्गीता | धर्मक्षेत्रे |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Normal <ScriptHelpLinks client:visible script="Normal" /> | Plain keyboard letters (ASCII) | `bhagavadgItA` | `dharmakShEtrE` |
| Romanized <ScriptHelpLinks client:visible script="Romanized" /> | Latin letters with diacritics | <span class="font-romanized">bhagavadgītā</span> | <span class="font-romanized">dharmakṣētrē</span> |

Romanized is Normal with diacritics: each Normal letter has exactly one Romanized form (`A` ā, `Sh` ṣ, `T` ṭ…).

### Normal

An ASCII scheme mixing [Harvard-Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard-Kyoto) and [ITRANS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITRANS), extended to all Indian scripts. It is case sensitive: long vowels and retroflex consonants are capitals (`A` आ, `T` ट, `N` ण), and aspirates add `h` (`kh` ख, `Th` ठ).

<ScriptHelpLinks client:visible script="Normal" labelled />

<Aside title="The one difference that matters most">
**Capital `E` and `O` are the long ए ओ; small `e` and `o` are the short ऎ ऒ.**

Sanskrit has only the long e and o, so Harvard-Kyoto and ITRANS use `e` and `o` for them and have nothing left for the short ones, which Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam and Tamil need. Telugu ఏడు (seven) is `EDu`, ఎనిమిది (eight) is `enimidi`, and Sanskrit देव is `dEva`.
</Aside>

<details>
<summary>Normal compared with Harvard-Kyoto and ITRANS</summary>

| Letter | Normal | Harvard-Kyoto | ITRANS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ए ఏ / ऎ ఎ (long / short e) | `E` / `e` | `e` (both) | `e` (both) |
| ओ ఓ / ऒ ఒ (long / short o) | `O` / `o` | `o` (both) | `o` (both) |
| च छ | `ch` `Ch` | `c` `ch` | `ch` `Ch` |
| श ष | `sh` `Sh` | `z` `S` | `sh` `Sh` |
| ऋ ॠ | `R` `RR` | `R` `RR` | `RRi` `RRI` |
| ऌ ॡ | `LR` `LRR` | `lR` `lRR` | `LLi` `LLI` |
| ङ ञ | `G` `J` | `G` `J` | `~N` `~n` |
| ँ (candrabindu) | `MM` | `~` | `.N` |
| ॐ | `AUM` | `OM` | `OM` |
| ऽ (avagraha) | `` ` `` | `'` | `.a` |
| । ॥ | `.` `..` | `.` `..` | `\|` `\|\|` |
| ॅ ॉ (as in डॉक्टर) | `aiI` `auU` | `e` `o` | `e` `o` |
| ழ / ற ன (Tamil) | `Lz` / `rz` `nz` | `zh` / `r2` `n2` | `zh` / `R` `n` |

Nukta letters add `z` (क़ `kz`, ज़ `jz`, फ़ `phz`), and Vedic accents are arrows (`↑` udātta, `↓` anudātta).

| Word | Normal | Harvard-Kyoto | ITRANS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| कृष्ण | `kRShNa` | `kRSNa` | `kRRiShNa` |
| पञ्च | `paJcha` | `paJca` | `pa~ncha` |
| सोऽहम् | ``sO`ham`` | `so'ham` | `so.aham` |
| Tamil தமிழ் | `tamiLz` | `tamizh` | `tamizh` |

<Aside type="tip" title="Typing is more forgiving">
Converting text reads only the forms above, so `raama` gives रअम. [Typing](/app) also accepts `aa` for आ, `ii` or `ee` for ई and `uu` or `oo` for ऊ.
</Aside>

</details>

### Romanized

The same letters as Normal with diacritics, close to [IAST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Alphabet_of_Sanskrit_Transliteration) and [ISO 15919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_15919). **Long e and o are ē and ō**, plain `e` `o` the short ones, as in Normal. For Sanskrit this only changes the look (<span class="font-romanized">dēva</span> for _deva_). It looks much like IAST but some letters differ, so convert IAST text from the **IAST** script.

<ScriptHelpLinks client:visible script="Romanized" labelled />

<details>
<summary>Romanized compared with IAST</summary>

| Letter | Romanized | IAST |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ए ओ / ऎ ऒ (long / short) | <span class="font-romanized">ē ō</span> / <span class="font-romanized">e o</span> | e o (both; è ò read as short) |
| च छ | <span class="font-romanized">ch Ch</span> | c ch |
| ं (anusvāra) | <span class="font-romanized">ṁ</span> (ṃ also read) | ṃ |
| ऌ ॡ | <span class="font-romanized">l̥ l̥̄</span> | ḷ ḹ |
| ळ / ऴ (ழ) | <span class="font-romanized">ḷ</span> / <span class="font-romanized">ḻ</span> | ḻ (both) |
| ॅ ॉ | <span class="font-romanized">ê ô</span> | e o |
| ऽ (avagraha) | <span class="font-romanized">`</span> | ' |
| क़ ज़ फ़ | <span class="font-romanized">kz jz phz</span> | q z f |

Watch two of these when converting: **ch** is च in Romanized but छ in IAST, and **ḷ** is ळ in Romanized but the vowel ऌ in IAST. IAST also ignores case, while in Romanized capitals carry meaning (`Ch` is छ), so write it in lowercase.

| Word | Romanized | IAST |
| --- | --- | --- |
| पञ्च | <span class="font-romanized">pañcha</span> | pañca |
| संस्कृतम् | <span class="font-romanized">saṁskṛtam</span> | saṃskṛtam |
| डॉक्टर | <span class="font-romanized">ḍôkṭara</span> | ḍokṭara |
| Malayalam മലയാളം | <span class="font-romanized">malayāḷaṁ</span> | malayāḻaṃ |

</details>

## Extended scripts

### Tamil-Extended

Tamil has one letter where Sanskrit has four: க is k, kh, g and gh alike. Tamil-Extended tells them apart with a superscript number:

| Sanskrit | क ka | ख kha | ग ga | घ gha |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tamil-Extended | <span class="font-tamil-extended">க</span> | <span class="font-tamil-extended">க²</span> | <span class="font-tamil-extended">க³</span> | <span class="font-tamil-extended">க⁴</span> |

<ScriptHelpLinks client:visible script="Tamil-Extended" labelled />

<details>
<summary>Examples: Tamil and Tamil-Extended compared</summary>

| Devanagari | Tamil | Tamil-Extended |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span class="font-devanagari">भगवद्गीता</span> | <span class="font-tamil">பகவத்கீதா</span> | <span class="font-tamil-extended">ப⁴க³வத்³கீ³தா</span> |
| <span class="font-devanagari">दुःख</span> | <span class="font-tamil">துஃக</span> | <span class="font-tamil-extended">து³ஃக²</span> |
| <span class="font-devanagari">कृष्ण</span> | <span class="font-tamil">கிருஷ்ண</span> | <span class="font-tamil-extended">க்ரு²ஷ்ண</span> |
| <span class="font-devanagari">सिंह</span> | <span class="font-tamil">ஸிம்ஹ</span> | <span class="font-tamil-extended">ஸிஂஹ</span> |

It also writes ऋ as <span class="font-tamil-extended">ரு²</span>, keeps the anusvāra as <span class="font-tamil-extended">ஂ</span> instead of <span class="font-tamil">ம்</span>, and can write Vedic accents.

</details>

### Purna-Devanagari

_Pūrṇa_ means complete: Devanagari with the letters needed to write any Indian script without losing a sound. Plain Devanagari has no short e and o, so Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam and Tamil text loses that difference in it.

<ScriptHelpLinks client:visible script="Purna-Devanagari" labelled />

<details>
<summary>What Purna-Devanagari adds, with examples</summary>

| Added | Letters | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short e, o | <span class="font-devanagari">ऎ ॆ ऒ ॊ</span> | Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Tamil |
| Kashmiri vowels | <span class="font-devanagari">ॳ ॴ ॵ ॶ ॷ</span> | Kashmiri |
| Sindhi consonants | <span class="font-devanagari">ॻ ॼ ॾ ॿ</span> | Sindhi |

| Telugu | Devanagari | Purna-Devanagari |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span class="font-telugu">ఎనిమిది</span> (eight) | <span class="font-devanagari">एनिमिदि</span> | <span class="font-devanagari">ऎनिमिदि</span> |
| <span class="font-telugu">ఒకటి</span> (one) | <span class="font-devanagari">ओकटि</span> | <span class="font-devanagari">ऒकटि</span> |

From Purna-Devanagari, ఎనిమిది converts back unchanged; from plain Devanagari it comes back as ఏనిమిది.

</details>

## Standard Romanization Schemes

Well-known schemes for Sanskrit in Latin letters. They follow their published standards, so they have no separate short e and o: Telugu ఎ and ఏ both become `e`.

| Script | भगवद्गीता | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IAST <ScriptHelpLinks client:visible script="IAST" /> | bhagavadgītā | Diacritics; capitals are read too (_Kṛṣṇa_), and è ò as short e, o |
| ITRANS <ScriptHelpLinks client:visible script="ITRANS" /> | `bhagavadgItA` | ASCII, ITRANS 5.3 with its alternate spellings (`kRRiShNa`, `kR^iShNa`) |
| Harvard-Kyoto <ScriptHelpLinks client:visible script="Harvard-Kyoto" /> | `bhagavadgItA` | ASCII, case sensitive (`kRSNa`) |
| SLP1 <ScriptHelpLinks client:visible script="SLP1" /> | `BagavadgItA` | ASCII, one letter per sound (`kfzRa`) |
